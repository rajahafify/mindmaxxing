### The three roles every company needs
- **Tags:** #team #hiring #startup #hormozi
- **What it is:** A business only needs three roles covered: a **Promoter** who sells the product, a **Builder** who makes the product, and an **Operator** who runs the day to day. One person can cover all three, or each role can be a team of 20.
- **Why it works:** It turns "who should I hire?" into "which role is uncovered?" Most stalled businesses are missing one of the three, like a great product nobody sells, or strong sales with delivery falling apart.
- **Example:** A solo founder starts as all three. The first hire is usually whichever role the founder is weakest at or hates most, often operations.
- **How I'd use it:** Audit any business or partnership by asking "who's the promoter, builder, and operator?" When picking co-founders, look for someone strong in a role I'm not.
- **Watch out:** One person holding all three roles can do it early on but burns out at scale. Overlap matters too: two builders and no promoter is a common startup failure.
- **Source:** Alex Hormozi (no link, shared from memory), noted 2026-10-28

### Signal vs. noise: 3 things in the next 18 hours
- **Tags:** #productivity #focus #priorities #kevin-oleary #steve-jobs
- **What it is:** Each day, pick the 3 things you must get done in the next 18 hours. That's the *signal*. Everything else, like emails, meetings, and busywork, is *noise*. Kevin O'Leary tells this as a lesson he got from Steve Jobs.
- **Why it works:** Having too many priorities means having none. A short, hard limit forces you to decide what actually moves the needle, instead of reacting to whatever is loudest. 18 hours is roughly one waking day plus the evening before, short enough to be urgent but long enough to finish real work. Three is a small enough number to keep in your head.
- **Example:** Tonight, write: (1) send proposal to Client X, (2) record this week's video, (3) fix the checkout bug. Tomorrow, do those before checking anything else. Anything that doesn't serve them waits.
- **How I'd use it:** Write the 3 the night before or first thing in the morning, then review at the end of the day. Ask about any new task, "is this signal or noise?"
- **Watch out:** Pick outcomes, not activities ("send the proposal", not "work on the proposal"). Some noise, like admin and replies, still needs a batch time slot so it doesn't pile up. And don't let urgent-but-unimportant tasks sneak into the top 3.
- **Source:** [Instagram reel, Kevin O'Leary](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeefnZ5todT/), found 2026-10-28
