### 2 Lego technique (let them snap it together)
- **Tags:** #self-persuasion #inception #storytelling #presentation
- **What it is:** Instead of stating your conclusion, present two separate, seemingly unrelated pieces (the "Lego bricks"). Leave the gap open so the listener connects them and arrives at your idea on their own.
- **Why it works:** People trust conclusions they reach themselves far more than ones they are told (self-persuasion, the generation effect). It is close to Aristotle's *enthymeme*, an argument with one premise left unsaid for the audience to fill in. It also slips past resistance, because there is no claim to push back against, and the "aha" moment makes the idea stick and feel like theirs.
- **Example (illustrative, made-up numbers):** Pitching a budget tool. Brick 1: "The average person spends 23 minutes a week checking their bank balance." Brick 2: "Our users check theirs once a month." You never say "our tool saves you time and stress", but the listener concludes it.
- **How I'd use it:** In pitches, sales calls, and content hooks, drop the punchline and let the audience land it. Works well for ideas that sound like bragging or salesy when said outright.
- **Watch out:** If the bricks are too far apart, people won't connect them, or they'll draw the wrong conclusion. Test it on someone first. Implying a conclusion that's false is still misleading.
